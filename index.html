Expedition #01 · Copy

+94% Form Submissions: Reframing Payroll Copy Around Legal Risk

Digital payroll form conversion nearly doubled (3.43% → 7.43%) after H2s were rewritten to lead with the pain and legal/compliance risk of manual payroll, instead of generic feature language.

+94%Form Submissions
RoleCRO Analyst — copy strategy, testing, analysis
FormatA/B Test
CategoryCopy
Funnel stageMQL
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

The digital payroll landing page communicated features and convenience, but visitors evaluating a payroll switch weren't primarily worried about convenience — they were worried about getting compliance wrong. Generic, benefit-led H2s weren't giving that concern anywhere to land, and form conversion sat around 3.43%.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If we rewrite the page's H2s to foreground the pain and legal/compliance risk of manual payroll instead of feature benefits, then visitors will feel the cost of inaction more concretely and submit the form at a higher rate.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Audited existing H2s and identified where messaging stayed abstract instead of naming a real, specific risk.
  • 2Rewrote H2s around the pain of manual payroll errors and the legal exposure of non-compliance.
  • 3Ran a controlled A/B test of the new copy against the original feature-led version.
Format: A/B TestCategory: Copy
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+94%Form submissions
3.43%Baseline conversion rate
7.43%Winning variant conversion rate

Business impact: more than doubling the form conversion rate meaningfully increased qualified top-of-funnel volume without any additional media spend.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

Risk-based framing outperforms feature framing for compliance-driven products.
Naming the cost of inaction can beat listing the benefits of action.
Next: extend the same pain/risk framing test to adjacent HR product pages.

Expedition #02 · Copy

+83% Form Conversion: Manual vs. Automated Comparative Copy

HR (Departamento Pessoal) form conversion grew 83% after headlines were rewritten to explicitly contrast the manual process visitors already live with the automated alternative.

+83%Form Conversion
RoleCRO Analyst — copy strategy, testing, analysis
FormatA/B Test
CategoryCopy
Funnel stageMQL
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

Visitors landing on the HR/DP page already understood their current process was manual and slow — the page just wasn't making that gap visible. Abstract value statements ("more efficient," "streamlined") didn't connect the dots to their day-to-day reality clearly enough to convert.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If H2s explicitly contrast the manual process the visitor already lives with the automated alternative, then the value will be immediately legible and more visitors will submit the form.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Mapped the manual DP process step by step to find the most painful, relatable moments.
  • 2Rewrote H2s as direct manual-vs-automated comparisons instead of abstract benefit claims.
  • 3A/B tested the comparative copy against the original page.
Format: A/B TestCategory: Copy
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+83%Form conversion
Before/AfterComparative framing
WinnerStatistically significant

Business impact: the comparative narrative became the go-to copy pattern for other HR-admin pages, since it reliably outperformed abstract value statements.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

Contrast is persuasive when the audience is already living the "before" state.
Comparative framing clarifies value faster than abstract benefit statements.
Next: template this before/after pattern for other admin-heavy product lines.

Expedition #03 · Wpp

+7.04% Site SALs: Expanding the Desktop WhatsApp Button

Sales-accepted leads originated from the site grew 7.04% after the desktop WhatsApp entry point was redesigned to expand with a short delay, drawing attention without feeling intrusive.

+7.04%SALs Originated from Site
RoleCRO Analyst — UX design, rollout, analysis
FormatDirect Implementation
CategoryWpp
Funnel stageSAL
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

On desktop, the WhatsApp entry point was a small, static icon easy to miss — especially compared to how naturally visible it felt on mobile. Desktop visitors with real intent to talk to the team were passing right by it.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If the desktop WhatsApp button visually expands with a short delay after page load, then more desktop visitors will notice it and start a qualified conversation, increasing SALs originated from the site.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Redesigned the desktop button into a "dynamic pill" with CTA text that expands after a short delay instead of staying a static icon, and the mobile button into a minimal "circle badge" to avoid obstructing small screens.
  • 2Tuned the delay and animation so the expansion felt like a helpful nudge, not a pop-up interruption.
  • 3Shipped directly to desktop given the strong directional signal and low downside risk, then monitored SAL volume by source.
Format: Direct ImplementationCategory: Wpp
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+7.04%SALs from site
DesktopChannel scope
Low-riskShip without full A/B

Business impact: a small, contained UI change turned an under-noticed channel into a measurably larger source of sales-accepted leads.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

Desktop users respond well to delayed, low-friction attention cues.
Not every improvement needs a full A/B test when directional risk is low and evidence is strong.
Next: test the same expand-with-delay pattern on other conversational entry points.

Expedition #04 · UX

+11% SALs: A Chatbot-Guided, Self-Serve Get Demo Experience

Sales-accepted leads grew 11% after the static "request a demo" form was replaced with a free interactive demo, guided end-to-end by a conversational chatbot flow.

+11%SALs
RoleCRO Analyst — UX flow, chatbot logic, testing
FormatSplit URL
CategoryUX
Funnel stageSAL
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

The original "Get Demo" flow was a form gate: fill in your details, wait for a sales rep to reach out, then see the product for the first time. Many visitors weren't ready to talk to sales before experiencing any value, and dropped off at the gate.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If we replace the static demo request with a free, interactive demo guided by a chatbot, then more visitors will experience the product's value before talking to sales — increasing both engagement and the number of sales-accepted leads.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Designed a self-serve interactive demo that let visitors explore key use cases hands-on.
  • 2Built a chatbot flow to guide users step by step through the demo, answering questions along the way.
  • 3Tested the new experience against the original demo-request form via a split URL.
Format: Split URLCategory: UX / Wpp
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+11%SALs
ChatbotGuided flow
Self-serveBefore sales contact

Business impact: leads reaching sales had already experienced the product, arriving warmer and better informed — improving both quantity and quality of the handoff.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

Letting users experience value before a sales call improves lead quality, not just volume.
Conversational guidance reduces the intimidation of a "cold" product tour.
Next: extend the chatbot flow to segment leads by use case before handoff to sales.

Expedition #05 · Forms

+12.5% SHOW Rate: Mandatory CNPJ, Minus 10 Columns

Meeting show-rate grew 12.5% after making the company tax ID (CNPJ) mandatory at the point of submission, while trimming ~10 non-predictive fields from the qualification form.

+12.5%SHOW Rate
RoleCRO Analyst — form strategy, testing, analysis
FormatSplit URL
CategoryForms
Funnel stageSHOW
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

The form generated volume, but a meaningful share of scheduled calls ended in no-shows — leads that looked qualified on paper weren't showing enough real buying intent to book time and keep it.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If we require a CNPJ at the point of submission — while removing other fields that don't actually predict fit — then leads will be better qualified and more likely to show up for the scheduled call.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Reviewed which of the existing ~10+ fields actually correlated with show-rate versus which just added friction.
  • 2Removed the non-predictive fields and made CNPJ mandatory as the single strongest qualification signal.
  • 3Ran the leaner, CNPJ-required version against the original form via a split URL.
Format: Split URLCategory: Forms
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+12.5%Show rate
-10 colsFields removed
1 fieldAdded: CNPJ (required)

Business impact: a leaner form both reduced friction and improved the odds that a booked meeting actually happened — a lagging but critical funnel metric.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

One well-chosen qualifying field can outperform many soft ones.
Show-rate is a lagging indicator of lead fit, not just of volume.
Next: explore CNPJ-based routing to tailor the pre-call experience by company size.

Expedition #06 · Trust

+15.6% Conversion: A "Trending" Social-Proof Tag on Rich Materials

Landing page conversion lifted 15.6% at ~99% statistical confidence after adding a lightweight "🔥 Trending" badge above the fold on gated-content landing pages.

+15.6%Conversion Rate Lift
RoleCRO Analyst — UX, testing, rollout
FormatA/B Test (~99% conf.)
CategoryTrust / UX
Funnel stageCR
Challenge Hypothesis Strategy Results → Learnings
01 · ChallengeProcess: Observe · Research

The challenge

Landing pages for gated rich materials (ebooks, templates, reports) relied entirely on the content's own appeal. Nothing on the page signaled that other people had already found this specific material valuable — a missing layer of social proof right where visitors decide whether to convert.

02 · HypothesisProcess: Analyze · Prioritize

Hypothesis

If we add a visible social-proof "Trending" tag in the first fold of the landing page, then visitors will trust the material more and both convert and engage with it at a higher rate.
03 · Strategy & ApplicationProcess: Experiment

Strategy & application

  • 1Designed a small "🔥 Em Alta / Trending" badge for the first fold of the LP template.
  • 2A/B tested the badge against the original layout, running until reaching ~99% statistical confidence.
  • 3Rolled the winning variant out to the top 10 highest-traffic landing pages.
Format: A/B TestCategory: Trust
04 · Results & Key MetricsProcess: Measure

What the data showed

+15.6%Conversion Lift (A/B)
+17.36%Engagement
Top 10 LPsRolled out

Business impact: a single, lightweight UI element moved both conversion and downstream engagement, and the statistical confidence was strong enough to justify an immediate roll-out beyond the original test pages.

05 · Learnings & Next StepsProcess: Learn · Scale

What stayed with me

Lightweight social proof can move both conversion and engagement at once.
High statistical confidence justifies fast roll-out beyond the original test set.
Next: test dynamic "Trending" tags driven by real-time download counts.
